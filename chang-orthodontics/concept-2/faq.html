Questions, answered plainly.

The things people actually ask at a first visit. If your question is not here, call the office and ask, or bring it to a free consultation.

Getting started

What ages do you treat?+

Everyone. Mostly kids and teens, and a growing number of adults. Treatment paths are tuned to each stage of growth.

When should my child first see an orthodontist?+

The American Association of Orthodontists recommends a first evaluation around age 7. Most children who come in at that age need nothing yet, but it tells us whether to treat, monitor, or do nothing.

Do I need a referral from my dentist?+

No. Many patients come to us through their dentist, but you can book a consultation directly.

What happens at the first visit?+

A free exam and digital scan, about 45 minutes. You leave understanding your options, your timeline, and your estimate, with no obligation.

Is the consultation really free?+

Yes. The exam, the 3D scan, and the written estimate cost nothing, whether or not you start treatment.

Choosing a treatment

Invisalign or braces?+

Both get excellent results in the right case. After your scan we show you a simulation of each and recommend the better fit for your goals. If your case genuinely does better with fixed braces, we will say so.

What kinds of braces do you offer?+

Metal, ceramic, and self-ligating systems. Ceramic blends with the tooth and is far less visible; metal is slightly more efficient for certain movements.

Do you handle complex cases?+

Yes. Dr. Chang is board-certified and takes referrals for cases involving significant bite correction, skeletal expansion, and retreatment after earlier orthodontics.

What is early or phase 1 treatment?+

Treatment done while a child is still growing, usually to create space or guide jaw development, so that later treatment is simpler or unnecessary.

What is airway-focused orthodontics?+

It considers how the jaw and palate relate to the nasal airway alongside alignment and bite. It is an evaluation lens rather than a separate product, and it is part of the standard exam here.

Cost and insurance

How much does treatment cost?+

It depends on the plan. Your free consultation ends with a clear, itemized estimate in writing before any decision.

Do you take insurance?+

We work with most major plans, verify your orthodontic benefit, and file on your behalf so the coordination is not your job.

Do you offer payment plans?+

Yes, arranged in house and matched to your budget rather than routed through a third party. The same plans apply at any age.

What if the treatment plan changes?+

Any change to scope or cost is reviewed and approved with you before it happens. The estimate you sign is the plan you are on.

During treatment

How long does treatment take?+

Most plans run 12 to 18 months. Your projected timeline comes from the 3D plan and is reviewed with you before anything starts.

How often are appointments?+

Usually every six to ten weeks, and they are short. Progress is checked against the plan each time.

Do you have after-school appointments?+

Yes. After-school slots are part of how the schedule is built, and we protect wait times so visits do not eat an afternoon.

Does it hurt?+

Expect pressure rather than pain for a few days after placement and after adjustments. Over-the-counter pain relief is usually enough.

What can I not eat with braces?+

Hard, sticky, and chewy foods are the usual culprits: ice, hard candy, caramel, nuts, and popcorn. You get a full list at placement.

What if a bracket comes loose or a wire pokes?+

Call us. Most of these are simple fixes, and we hold same-day slots for existing patients with active problems.

After treatment

Will I need a retainer?+

Yes, always. Teeth drift when nothing holds them, and retention is what decides whether the result lasts.

How long do I wear it?+

Full-time for several months, then nights indefinitely. Retention is not a phase that ends.

What if my teeth shift years later?+

Come in. If we have your scan on file, a replacement retainer is quick, and limited retreatment is often much smaller than people expect.
